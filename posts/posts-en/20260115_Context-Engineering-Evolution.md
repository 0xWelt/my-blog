AGI has never been the closed-door creation of any single company, but a two-way journey between ecosystem and intelligence. In this journey, "Context" is evolving from a technical concept into an evolutionary theory co-authored by the entire community. It is not only the window through which models understand the world, but also the canvas on which the ecosystem expands the boundaries of intelligence.

Today, we stand at yet another inflection point. As the community begins to provide structured, scenario-based Context for AI, rather than simple Prompts or tools, the evolutionary trajectory of AGI is being quietly rewritten. Let us first look back at the path traveled, and see how Context management strategies have arrived at where they are today.

## Chapter 1: The Age of Incantations - The Naive Exploration of System Prompt and User Input

It all began in the winter of late 2022 that ignited the world. The first-generation ChatGPT system was built on a simple binary structure of "System Prompt + User Input," which defined the most fundamental pattern of human-computer interaction. The community soon discovered that through carefully designed "magic spells" — those lengthy and precise User Prompts — models could demonstrate stunning performance beyond their basic capabilities in specific domains.

A typical case was the once-popular "Academic GPT": users needed to input lengthy instructions, requiring the model to play the role of an academic paper polishing expert, stipulating dozens of detailed rules from grammar and logic to citation formats. These Prompts were like the recipes of alchemists, passed down by word of mouth within the community.

But the ceiling of this approach soon became apparent:

- **Low capability boundary**: At that time, the model's Instruction Following ability was still in its infancy, and complex instructions often went awry
- **Poor transferability**: "Spells" tuned for Model A often failed on Model B, with each new model meaning starting over from scratch
- **Fragmented experience**: Users needed to memorize and input large amounts of templates, making interaction inherently cumbersome

The legacy of this stage is nearly blank, but it left one crucial insight: models need more structured capability guidance, not fragmented text incantations.

## Chapter 2: The Advance of Workflow - The Industrial Attempt at Fixed Agent Workflow

In 2023, platforms like Dify.ai, Coze, and LlamaIndex rose to prominence, bringing the new paradigm of "fixed workflow." Developers built multi-turn Agents and designed tool-call chains for specific scenarios through visual interfaces, paired with RAG external knowledge bases, truly bringing LLMs into the Agent era.

This was a qualitative leap: models moved from single-turn conversations to long-term tasks, from pure text interaction to tool invocation. The community built countless Agent products based on this, from open-source automated operations assistants to closed-source industry knowledge base Q&A systems. Workflow technology allowed LLMs to run longer and gain more interactive capabilities.

However, its flaws were equally obvious:

- **Case-by-case customization**: Each feature required an entire heavy workflow; deploying a complete ordering system just to buy a cup of coffee
- **High migration costs**: Workflows optimized for GPT-4 might perform very differently on Claude
- **Lack of flexibility**: Once a process was solidified, it became difficult to respond to the dynamic changes of the real world

Nevertheless, this stage left valuable cognitive assets:

- Multiple interactions can exceed the capability ceiling of a single LLM — the paradox of "more artificial, more intelligent" manifests here
- Agent Workflow did not disappear, but evolved. Today we use harnesses or scaffolds to express similar concepts, but grant Agents greater autonomous decision-making space

## Chapter 3: The MCP Revolution - The Tool Carnival of Dynamic Agents

From late 2024 to early 2025, a quiet revolution swept in with the MCP (Model Control Protocol) standard. This was not an entirely new concept — OpenAI had defined Tool Declaration and Tool Call specifications as early as June 2023 — but MCP truly drove the cost of tool implementation to rock bottom.

The community responded explosively: a large number of digital world APIs were quickly aligned to the MCP format, from GitHub operations to food delivery orders, from database queries to smart home controls. A vast MCP market took shape instantly. Meanwhile, base models represented by Claude demonstrated powerful multi-step toolcall capabilities, able to autonomously iterate and invoke tools to complete complex tasks.

The shackles of Fixed Workflow were broken: Agents no longer needed pre-set rigid processes, but could dynamically plan paths according to goals. Capability boundaries and generalizability improved simultaneously, making AGI seem within reach.

But problems soon emerged:

- **Pseudo-demand flooding**: MCP is essentially just a format wrapper for APIs. For scenarios with existing APIs, development costs are extremely low, leading to the absurd situation where "there are more developers than users"
- **AGI hallucination**: The community fell into the delusion that "connecting enough MCPs can achieve AGI," ignoring the capability ceiling of the models themselves
- **Context pollution**: Flat abuse of tool declarations led to context disasters, with hundreds of tool definitions crowding the window, severely challenging the model's Instruction Following capabilities. To mitigate this, mainstream Agent frameworks began limiting tool numbers — the Cursor editor once set the upper limit at 80

Crisis breeds wisdom. The experience of this stage profoundly changed subsequent design philosophy:

- Unified standards can unleash the洪荒之力 of the community — standardization is the cornerstone of ecosystem prosperity
- Context management must be refined — the emergence of simple strategies like "hide tool result" foreshadowed more systematic Context exit mechanisms

## Chapter 4: The Rise of Skills - The Dimensional War of Context Management

When the flat exposure of MCP reached its limit, Skills emerged. This is not a simple reorganization of tools, but a dimensional upgrade of Context management strategy.

Why are Skills better? They achieve a triple breakthrough:

- **Hierarchical exposure**: Organizing flat tool lists into tree structures, expanding on demand, avoiding Context pollution while saving precious context window space
- **Proactive Context**: Models autonomously decide when and which layer of Context to acquire, rather than passively receiving all information
- **SOP-level instructions**: Not just "what can be done" (MCP's "I have an XX tool"), but "how to do it" — built-in detailed standard operating procedures, equivalent to applying intelligent patches to tools

More颠覆性的是, many Skills are completely defined in natural language, without programming. This greatly lowers the barrier to community participation, allowing business experts rather than programmers to contribute Context assets to the ecosystem. To some extent, this is a revival and transcendence of the second-stage Agent Workflow — using the flexibility of natural language to resurrect the structural advantages of workflow.

But this also poses new challenges for models:

- **Proactive context control capability**: Can the model still remember a certain Skill that was initially prompted after dozens of rounds of tool calls?
- **Long-context memory capability**: How to maintain awareness of high-level Context in deep call chains?

The lessons are already clear:

- Context must be hierarchical + proactively exposed
- What the community co-builds is not just tools, but process knowledge — SOPs, best practices, domain methodologies

## Ongoing Trends: The Refined Evolution of Context Management

From the above evolutionary history, we can clearly see three continuous threads:

### 1. From Flat to Hierarchical: The Victory of Scalability

The era of passively acquiring all Context is ending. More refined, more economical, and more scalable hierarchical exposure is becoming mainstream. This is not only engineering optimization, but a paradigm shift in cognition — Context acquisition should be an information foraging, not an information bombardment.

### 2. Continuous Improvement of Model Autonomy

The Bitter Lesson continues to exert force here: as base model intelligence enhances and Instruction Following capabilities continuously break through, developers can define more complex structures, and models can understand and execute them. Agent Workflow is becoming "less structure" — evolving from rigid processes requiring code definition to flexible Skills described in natural language. Less structure, more intelligence.

### 3. Continuously Lowering Community Participation Barriers

The breadth of the ecosystem determines the height of intelligence. When contributing Context no longer requires programming ability, when business experts can directly define Skills in natural language, the community's creativity is completely unleashed. This democratization process is rapidly expanding the capability boundaries of base models.

## Next Chapter: From Predefined to Scenario-Native

But the story is not over. New trends are budding, and they will reshape the way Context is supplied.

### Trend 1: From "Predefined" to "Scenario-Provided"

Past Context was all predefined: developers wrote MCP servers, packaged Skills, and users manually selected and loaded them. Future Context will be proactively provided by scenarios.

Imagine a world: coffee shop operators no longer need to develop Apps or mini-programs, but implement a "Context interface" — when a user's Agent enters a geofence or scans an in-store QR code, it automatically receives the Context package for that scenario. This Context includes:

- Menu and recommendations (no longer GUIs for humans, but structured data for Agents)
- Ordering protocols (natural language SOPs similar to Skills)
- Payment processes
- Wait time models

From making GUIs for humans to making Context protocols for Agents — this is a fundamental shift in digital world interaction paradigms.

### Trend 2: From "Only Enter, Never Exit" to "Flexible Exit"

Current Context management is basically one-way: load, load, and load again until the window explodes. What is needed in the future is something like Python's `with` statement context manager: defining both entry and exit.

When a user leaves a coffee shop scenario, the Agent should automatically:

- Archive detailed interaction logs ("ordered a latte, caramel switched to vanilla, less ice")
- Remove the scenario's MCP/Skills from active Context
- Retain only a condensed summary ("spent 35 yuan at Blue Bottle Coffee")

This Context exit mechanism can both avoid long-term pollution and achieve precise memory, making it an essential capability for long-running Agent systems.

## A Cup of Coffee to See the Future

Using a simple example to connect the old and new worlds:

**Old Paradigm**:

Enter coffee shop → Scan QR code → Jump to mini-program/H5 page → User manually orders and pays

**New Paradigm**:

Enter coffee shop → Agent automatically discovers scenario Context → Proactively informs user: "I detected the menu of Blue Bottle Coffee, would you like me to introduce it?" → User in natural language: "A less-ice Americano, Ethiopian beans" → Agent autonomously completes selection, customization, ordering, payment → Predicts wait time and sets reminder → Automatically cleans up Context after leaving, retaining only consumption records

Users return to pure intent expression, with all intermediate processes autonomously completed by the Agent under the guidance of scenario-based Context.

## Conclusion: Toward a Context-Native Intelligent Ecosystem

From incantations to Workflow, from MCP to Skills, and then to scenario-native Context, we see a clear thread: Context is evolving from an attachment to human input into symbiotic infrastructure for the ecosystem.

The password to AGI does not lie in the parameter count of some closed-source model, but in whether the community can build a context-rich, dynamic, scenario-based digital world. When every coffee shop, every library, every laboratory provides native Context for AI, intelligence will truly "emerge."

This is not only the evolution of technology, but also a revolution in collaboration paradigms. From developers outnumbering users, to scenario participants outnumbering developers, the next chapter of the ecosystem will be co-authored by all maintainers of digital spaces.

**Context is no longer a problem for models to handle, but an answer the world provides for intelligence.**
